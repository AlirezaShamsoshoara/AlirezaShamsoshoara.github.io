### 2. AllocFlow: End-to-End Portfolio Trading Action Generation from Raw Market Data
**Authors:** Annan Wang, Yuchen Xie, Yu Cen, Leon Liangyu Chen, Qianyu Julie Zhu, Xin Li, Tianyi Wei, Weisi Lin
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.19937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19937)
**Summary:** AllocFlow directly generates joint portfolio allocations from raw multi-asset market histories using a cross-asset flow Transformer, strategy-supervised imitation, and group-relative reinforcement learning. In the reported cryptocurrency experiments, RL refinement raises annualized net return from 27.79% to 51.38% and the Calmar ratio from 0.955 to 4.043, with further evaluation on unseen data without retraining.

### 4. InclusionMed: Benchmarking AI for Inclusive Healthcare
**Authors:** The InclusionMed Collaboration
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.21218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21218)
**Summary:** InclusionMed organizes medical AI evaluation across healthcare roles and capabilities, initially combining four audited public benchmarks with a private diagnostic-reasoning benchmark. Its evaluation finds substantial variation in model rankings by role and capability, stronger patient communication than clinical decision-making among the top models, and differing inference costs for similarly scoring models.

### 5. SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection
**Authors:** Woosang Lim, Yeseul Chang, Jaewoo Lee
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.07222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07222)
**Summary:** SPILLWAY separates deterministic admission of action targets from sanitization, allowing newly encountered targets only when supported by typed read-only tool returns or fixed delegation rules. Across the reported agent evaluations, it blocks standard templated redirection attacks and achieves a mean attack success rate of 0.005 under a released RL attacker with almost no additional model calls.

### 6. SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations
**Authors:** Xinping Lei, Minghao Liu, Ruijie Zhu, Xiaohui Hu, Xingqiao Li, Ao Zhang, Wenhao Chai, Han Li, Tao Wang, Jingyuan Zhang, Enduo Zhao, Yunfeng Shi, Yiming Liang, Zetao Yang, Xingjian Wang, Yifan Yao, Xinkai Ma, Yingru Li, Tianhan Yang, Huan Zhou, Weiran Shi, Shen Yan, Jiaheng Liu, Stephen Huang, Eli Cheung, Weihao Xuan
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20218)
**Summary:** SWE-CoWork evaluates coding agents on 101 assignments in real repositories within simulated organizations, requiring them to recover distributed requirements, collaborate, implement changes, and pass private tests. The leading models satisfy about 63% of fail-to-pass tests but complete fewer than 8% of assignments, exposing gaps in sustained requirement tracking and verified delivery.

### 7. ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters
**Authors:** Ruixi Yu, Dairu Liu, Xuchuan Chen, Zekun Qi, Zekai Li, Zhuo Cao, Luyang Niu, Yingsheng Zhang, Sikai Liang, Ke Fan, He Wang, Li Yi
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.21607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21607)
**Summary:** ScaleVLA connects a 552B vision-language backbone to a trainable action diffusion Transformer and compares backbones from 2B to 552B under a shared robot-learning setup. It reports improved sample efficiency, generalization, and real-world charger insertion, while maintaining 216 ms median latency per action chunk.

### 8. PaperCritic: Training AI Reviewers to Assess the Soundness of Papers
**Authors:** Hyun Ryu, Ji Yong Cho, Eunho Yang, Moontae Lee, June Yong Yang, Sean Welleck, Carolyn Rose
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.17834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17834)
**Summary:** PaperCritic focuses AI reviewing on scientific soundness, using a 23K-point quality-labeled dataset and reinforcement learning that rewards both precision and recall of substantive review points. It outperforms the compared frontier models and reviewer-training methods on its in-domain test set and transfers to Nature-family papers and other reviewing tasks.

### 9. VoxMel TTS Technical Report
**Authors:** Wenhao Guan, Jiaxin Ye, Haitao Li, Yifan Duan, Junxi Liu, Wangjin Zhou, Lin Li, Qingyang Hong, Xie Chen
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.20254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20254)
**Summary:** VoxMel is a tokenizer-free autoregressive TTS framework that uses an exactly invertible Haar transform to separate coarse mel-spectrogram generation from non-autoregressive residual reconstruction. Trained on 100K hours, its 0.6B model achieves strong English and Chinese speech metrics, improves further with GRPO, and supports two- or four-step distilled flow heads.

### 10. Accelerate Bench: Evaluating Agents for Application-Level Program Optimization
**Authors:** Bowen Liu, Yuhan Li, Nuo Chen, Weile Luo, Miao Peng, Qintong Zhang, Qifan Zhang, Jia Li, Ziqi Gao, Lydell Huang
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.20882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20882)
**Summary:** Accelerate Bench evaluates coding agents on 32 complete CPU optimization tasks with explicit correctness requirements, timing boundaries, permitted edits, and a separate final verifier. Results across 16 agents show that speedup, rather than correctness alone, distinguishes performance and that build settings and interacting code changes strongly affect application-level gains.

### 11. eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation
**Authors:** Tong Zhao, Xianfang Zeng, Yucheng Han, Ruoyu Wang, Mingkun Lei, Gang Yu, Chi Zhang
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194)
**Summary:** Endpoint Distribution Matching Distillation trains its auxiliary fake-score model only on final student outputs and uses that endpoint reference to correct every denoising step. The authors prove a matching condition independent of intermediate distributions and report better four-step generation than existing distribution-matching methods across several image models.

### 12. SALA: Semantically Aligned Latent Actions Enable Dual-Axis Scaling of Embodied Foundation Models
**Authors:** Jiachun Jin, Zetong Zhou, Haonan Wang, Zhijie Deng
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.17110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17110)
**Summary:** SALA aligns latent actions from unpaired human and robot videos using language-derived semantic similarities, while robot action reconstruction grounds the shared space in control. Increasing general human video during pre-training reduces robot action prediction error, and increasing task-specific human video during post-training improves real-world robot success.

### 13. OneSteer: Unlocking Steerable General Humanoid Motion Tracking via One-Step Diffusion Whole-Body Control
**Authors:** Yifei Huang, Yazhao Yang, Li Weijia, Tianle Shi, Ji Jin, Zhihui Cai, Zihao Liu, Jianzhuang Zhao, Songlin Dong, Xinyuan Chang, Yihong Gong, Dian Shen, Zhiheng Ma
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2610.41354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41354)
**Summary:** OneSteer combines general humanoid motion tracking with test-time steering by optimizing the input noise of a frozen one-step diffusion whole-body controller using differentiable task costs. It completes all 27 nominal tracking trials on a physical Unitree G1 and demonstrates onboard-LiDAR-guided low-bar and hurdle traversal, alongside strong simulated steering results.

### 14. Calpha: Toward Native Biomolecular Intelligence in a General-Purpose Language Model
**Authors:** Yong Liu, Zhanpeng Shi, Peicong Lin, Zhenyi Zhong, Yizhou Dang, Wendi Wu, Haoling Li, Shuangjia Zheng
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.20986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20986)
**Summary:** Calpha connects Qwen3.5-9B residue states to a pretrained structure head so a general-purpose language model can output single-chain protein structures. The initial system shows competitive short-chain comparisons but lower long-chain scores and reduced accuracy on the evaluated MMLU-Pro subset, with complex prediction and reasoning-guided structure changes left for future work.

### 15. Interpreting Coding Agents Through Their Behavioral Trajectories
**Authors:** Ibragim Badertdinov, Alexander Golubev
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.17440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17440)
**Summary:** The authors analyze 143,209 coding-agent trajectories from 125 configurations using a deterministic parser that maps heterogeneous traces into high-level search, editing, and testing actions. They find distinct behavioral signatures even at similar solve rates and systematic effects of scaffolding and reasoning effort, releasing the parser and analysis pipeline.

### 16. Continual-ARC: Measuring Skill Acquisition over a Working Lifetime
**Authors:** Daniel Lenton, Yusha Arif, Haris Mahmood
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505)
**Summary:** Continual-ARC creates a lifelong stream from the three ARC-AGI editions, with recurring tasks, fresh instances, and a shared paid-feedback budget for memory-based and weight-based learners. In the reported experiments, persistent frozen-weight harnesses generally fail to beat a simple demonstration cache or generalize to unseen tasks, while weight-based learning shows substantial gains.

### 17. MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos
**Authors:** Yunyang Ge, Weijie Kong, Ryan Xu, Yue Wu, Qi Tian, Jiangfeng Xiong, Bin Lin, Mingzhe Zheng, Shenghai Yuan, Zheyuan Liu, Jianwei Zhang, Li Yuan
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.21339](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21339)
**Summary:** MovieRoll connects streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference using window-wise teacher forcing, hierarchical KV-cache compression, and cumulative long tuning. It reduces cache memory by up to 78% while improving quality, and its distilled model achieves the highest compared HeliosBench totals across evaluations extending to 60 seconds.

### 18. Re-Search: Dissecting LLM Ideation Through a Two-Player Questioning Game
**Authors:** Muhan Gao, Hangxiao Zhu, Xinqi Su, Yu Zhang, Daniel Khashabi, Kuan-Hao Huang
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.14487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14487)
**Summary:** Re-Search turns research ideation into a yes-or-no questioning game built from 200 ICML papers, tracking how models rediscover a paper's core idea without executing it. Across six models, progress usually plateaus after the first 30 questions, and interventions identify candidate generation rather than judgment as the main bottleneck.

### 19. Latent Feedback Is a Compute and Data Amplifier
**Authors:** Benhao Huang, Chaitanya Dwivedi, Xuezhe Ma, Alexander Moreno, Hector Liu
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.20404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20404)
**Summary:** This study compares latent-feedback models with tuned vanilla Transformers at matched compute across model sizes, unique-data amounts, and repeated training epochs. Latent feedback saves up to 48% of compute and 47% of unique data in the tested regimes, with a fitted scaling law predicting held-out larger-model losses accurately.

### 20. MemInStream: Memorizing or Merely Recognizing? Rethinking Streaming Video Memory Evaluation
**Authors:** Zhenqi He, Dunyuan Xu, Yang Bo, Kien T. Pham, Yanghao Wang, Shuhan Li, Xintong Guo, Yuyang He, Fang Liu, Huankang Guan, Jingyan Chen, Rui Liu, Long Chen
**arXiv:** [arxiv.org/abs/2611.15640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15640)
**Summary:** MemInStream evaluates streaming-video memory using evidence-localized questions that models can answer from the relevant evidence but not from recent observations, later observations, or text alone. Its four categories distinguish episodic retrieval, state tracking, multi-evidence integration, and contextual memory while reducing shortcuts that confound memory with visual understanding.
